A True Fiduciary, Not A Brokerage

How a review of unsuitable, illiquid fund investments became the founding story of Leppla Capital and the structures we still use today.

Introduction

In 2017, Bruce Leppla was introduced to the leadership of the California Ironworkers District Council (“DCIW”). The DCIW and Local Union Ironworkers funds were being managed in ways that did not serve their Risk Tolerances and Liquidity Needs.

The Problem

In many cases, the Ironworker funds were being managed by brokerage offices, and not by an investment management fiduciary, legally obligated to put client interests ahead of their own interests. The result: The Ironworker funds often were invested in high-fee, highly speculative, long-duration, illiquid, structured investments. They provided little to no current income to fund operating expenses. Returns materially trailed broad U.S. equity market returns. When the funds tried to sell the investments, there were no takers.

Our Advice

Leppla Capital, by contrast, is a valued fiduciary to all clients. Our advice was twofold: 1) Move the Ironworker accounts to a Registered Investment Adviser organized and operating as a client fiduciary, not a broker; and 2) Invest primarily in U.S. Treasury or Agency securities for fixed income, and “market-beta,” low-fee mutual funds or ETFs for equities.

What Followed

The relationship began with investment consulting contracts: first with the District Council of Ironworkers, and then with other Ironworker funds. Over time, that work led to a result that served both sides. Leppla Capital was asked to take over investment management for part of the District Council of Ironworkers and many of the related Local Funds.

That origin still shapes the firm. Clear mandates. Liquid building blocks. Fiduciary duty. Published economics. The same principles guide Layered Cash Management and the Securities Investment Pyramid™ today.

What We Are Today

Leppla Capital remains a boutique Registered Investment Adviser, based in San Francisco and New York. We begin a client relationship with understanding the client's cash flow requirements, liquidity requirements, and short and long-term investment risk tolerance and expected returns. Longer reserves sit in a separate account with a written risk framework.

The firm is led by people with different strengths: investment and fiduciary structure, operational discipline, and behavioral judgment. That mix is how a small team stays useful to unions, foundations, endowments, businesses, trusts, pension plans, and individuals who need clarity, not complexity.
